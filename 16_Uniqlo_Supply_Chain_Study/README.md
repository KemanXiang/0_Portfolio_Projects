# Global Supply Chain Configuration and Supplier Governance

## An Empirical Case Study of UNIQLO

*Strategic Sourcing, Operational Integration, Global Expansion, and Social Sustainability*

**Author:** Keman Xiang  
**Research areas:** Supply Chain Management, Operations Management, Strategic Management, Social Sustainability  
**Methods:** Theory-informed qualitative case study and Excel-based descriptive empirical analysis  

## 1. Project Overview

This research examines how **UNIQLO** configures, coordinates, and governs its **global supply chain** to support international expansion while balancing cost efficiency, product quality, operational responsiveness, and social sustainability. It integrates academic literature, documented corporate practices, independent labor assessments, and descriptive analysis of 578 production facility records disclosed by Fast Retailing.

The study considers sourcing, manufacturing, supplier relationships, logistics, and retail distribution as interconnected management decisions. It also includes the role of supplier development, responsible purchasing, and human rights due diligence in coordinating outsourced production.

The conceptual framework links strategic and institutional drivers to supply chain configuration and supplier governance, then to organizational and operational mechanisms supporting commercial and social objectives.

The research uses a qualitative-dominant single-case design. Document analysis and theory-informed interpretation are combined with descriptive statistics to examine the production network. Corporate statements, independent assessments, and theoretical interpretations are distinguished in the report.

### Excel analytical workflow

1. Preserve the source dataset and data cleaning.
2. Combine garment and processing facility records while retaining source-row identifiers.
3. Distinguish undisclosed information from structurally unavailable fields; avoid imputation.
4. Calculate geographic concentration, product composition, workforce distributions, and parent-label statistics.
5. Visualize results and dashborading.
6. Integrate quantitative evidence with qualitative findings and evaluate strategic alternatives.

All quantitative analysis is contained in the Excel workbook.

## 7. Strategic Evaluation and Managerial Implications

The research compares core supplier concentration, selective geographic diversification, and regional sourcing alongside direct-to-consumer (DTC), franchising, and wholesale distribution alternatives. The evaluation considers cost, quality, responsiveness, scalability, risk, and social sustainability without assigning unsupported numerical scores or financial returns.

|Recommendation|Managerial implication|
|---|---|
|Selective geographic diversification|Retain established supplier capabilities while screening qualified alternatives and assessing regional sourcing feasibility.|
|Supplier capability development|Extend technical coaching and joint improvement practices.|
|DTC-led omnichannel expansion|Coordinate retail and online fulfillment, localized assortment, and demand feedback.|
|Responsible purchasing integration|Align order placement, pricing, payment, and capacity plans with labor-risk prevention.|
|Multi-tier human rights governance|Strengthen traceability, worker feedback, remediation, and responsible supplier exit assessment.|

These recommendations are conditional managerial judgments, not measured intervention effects or optimized investment decisions.

## 9. Limitations and Future Research

The study relies on a single case and secondary evidence, including a cross-sectional group-level facility register. The register lacks procurement volumes, exact employee counts, verified ownership relationships, transaction links, and matched labor outcomes. Some academic full texts were inaccessible. Consequently, the research does not estimate causal effects, longitudinal network changes, actual purchasing exposure, or the effectiveness of labor interventions. Future research could combine purchasing histories, verified supplier identities, worker-level evidence, and operational performance across time.
